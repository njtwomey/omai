# Probabilistic inference (part 4, principles of learning)

Inference watched: graphical models and exact inference (variable elimination, belief propagation on trees, junction trees), Markov chain Monte Carlo (Metropolis, Gibbs, HMC, NUTS, slice, with diagnostics), variational inference (mean field, CAVI, stochastic VI, black-box VI, normalising-flow posteriors), message-passing approximations (loopy BP, expectation propagation), probabilistic programming, skill rating and model-based machine learning, as sessions over `pgm`, `sampling`, `probprog` and `dists`. The hidden Markov model, topic models and other named probabilistic models sit here too.

## Structure

Everything here answers a query about a model, so it lives under `infer/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`): `graphical/` for models given as graphs, `samplers/` and `variational/` for the two approximate families on any target, `programs/` for a probabilistic program as the model slot, `models/` for the named models users expect by name, and `filters/` for the sequential filters.

The sequence models are the question. A hidden Markov model is a Bayesian network (a dynamic one, unrolled), a linear-chain CRF is a conditional Markov random field, and both are inferred by forward-backward, which is belief propagation on a chain; dynamic Bayesian networks and linear dynamical systems are the same chain with richer slices. Filing them under `bayesian-networks/` and `markov-random-fields/` by their algebra would scatter one algorithm (`pgm/inference/chain.ts`) over two folders and hide what makes them a family: time. So `graphical/` is organised by what the graph looks like, with a `chains/` folder for the sequence models beside the general ones, and `message-passing/` for the algorithms themselves watched on any model:

```
infer/
  graphical/
    bayesian-networks/        variable elimination, junction trees, enumeration, sampling inference, parameter and structure learning, d-separation, plates, influence diagrams
    markov-random-fields/     Ising and Potts, Gaussian MRFs, the image-denoising MRF (MAP by ICM, max-product, MPLP)
    chains/
      hidden-markov-models/   forward-backward and Viterbi watched, Baum-Welch, Bayesian HMMs by FFBS, the variants
      conditional-random-fields/  linear-chain CRFs trained by any first-order method, general CRFs on grids
      dynamic-bayesian-networks/  2TBN unrolling, the interface algorithm, linear dynamical systems
    message-passing/          sum-product on a tree, loopy schedules and damping, mean field and VMP, TRW, expectation propagation
  samplers/                   MCMC with the kernel as a mode (built), the HMC trajectory, adaptation, SMC and tempering, importance sampling, SVGD, diagnostics
  variational/                CAVI on mixtures (built), SVI with guides as modes, Laplace, Gaussian EP, EM on programs
  programs/                   a program as the model slot run by any engine, the planner's ranking, exact engines, ABC, model criticism
  models/                     topic models, conjugate models, rating systems, item response theory, hierarchical models, Bayesian regression (built), online Bayesian classifiers
  filters/                    Kalman tracking (built), particle filters; the structural time-series forms are `fit/forecast/state-space/`
  shared/                     targets, the sampler and engine slots, named networks and programs, labelled sequences, the message overlay (below)
```

The alternative, flat `graphical/{bayesian-networks, markov-random-fields, conditional-random-fields, hidden-markov-models, message-passing}`, is one level shorter and loses only the grouping of HMMs with CRFs and DBNs; either is a lookup from this table. The neural sequence models (recurrent networks, state-space sequence models, transformers) are `train/sequence/` and the `sequence-models` chapter: "sequence model" means two things, and the chain here is the probabilistic one.

The built `methods/sequence/hmm.ts` moves to `chains/hidden-markov-models/`, `methods/sampling/mcmc.ts` to `samplers/`, `methods/mixture/bgmm.ts` is shared with `fit/cluster/` and appears in `variational/` as the CAVI exemplar, `methods/linear/bayesianRegression.ts` is the conjugate mode of `models/bayesian-regression`, and `methods/dynamics/kalmanTracking.ts` goes to `filters/`.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. Inference algorithms in `pgm` emit `message` events already (one per message passed), so the message-on-edge overlay is the same track everywhere: `messages/sent` m with the edge and its value.

## Problems

- `sampling/mcmc`, `sequence/hmm` and the named models move here. Probabilistic programs as the model slot need the program's latent names as paths on the tape: the contract becomes a function of the model (`site/<name>` above), which `defineMethod`'s `contract(c, key)` allows once the program is part of the configuration.
- Two sequence-model worlds: the chains here and the neural ones in `train/sequence/`; a page that compares an HMM with an LSTM on one labelled sequence spans both and belongs to `study/`.
- The library gaps are small and named: the online CRF trainers, HMM variants, Swendsen-Wang, the rating systems and IRT; the chain recursions as stepped algorithms.
