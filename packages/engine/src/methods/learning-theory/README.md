# Learning theory (part 4, principles of learning)

Learning theory simulated: PAC bounds against the observed generalisation gap, uniform convergence and capacity (shattering, the growth function, VC dimension, empirical Rademacher complexity), margins and norms, algorithm-dependent bounds (stability, PAC-Bayes, online regret), rates and consistency, no free lunch, bias-variance and double descent, implicit regularisation and lazy training, concentration bounds against tails, and the selection effects (optimism, degrees of freedom, James-Stein, adaptive data analysis), as sessions over the models of `supervised-learning` with `datasets` that have a known Bayes rate. Every method here is a study by repetition: fresh samples, a fit, a measurement, repeated, with the bound drawn beside the measurement.

## Structure

Everything here is a method over methods (a repeated-fit study of a model class) and lives under `study/theory/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`), one folder per family of result, taking the model class as its inner slot (a task method of `fit/` with its modes, or a hand-written class of `shared/classes.ts` such as thresholds, intervals, half-planes and polynomials of degree d) and the data slot with its Bayes rate. The repeated-sample base is shared with `statistics` (`shared/repeat.ts` there) and the sweep harness with `model-selection`; the selection effects (nested cross-validation, Thresholdout) are `model-selection`'s and appear as `see` rows.

```
study/theory/
  bounds/             the generalisation gap against the PAC, VC, Rademacher and margin bounds as n grows; concentration bounds against tails
  capacity/           shattering as a board, the growth function, empirical Rademacher complexity by simulation
  algorithmic/        stability of ridge, lasso and SGD, PAC-Bayes for stochastic predictors, online regret against its bound
  rates/              an estimator's error against n with its theoretical slope, consistency, no free lunch, the curse of dimensionality
  bias-variance/      the decomposition by repeated fits, double descent in random-feature regression, benign overfitting, lazy training and the NTK
  selection/          optimism and effective degrees of freedom, James-Stein's risk, multiple comparisons and p-hacking, information-theoretic bounds
  shared/             the hypothesis classes with their capacities, the bound catalogue, the repeated-fit base, the many-fits record (below)
```

Nothing of this chapter is built; its library needs are concentrated in one new file (`learn/theory.ts`, the bounds as functions) and the repeated-fit base.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. The step is one fresh sample and fit (the gap it produced, the fit itself kept faintly) or one value of the swept quantity (n, the degree, the width) with many fits inside it as sub-runs; the summary over the repeats is a projection. Every row shares `fit/trainError` s, `fit/testError` s, `fit/gap` s (the generalisation gap of the viewed fit), `study/curve` proj (the quantity against the swept knob, with its band) and `study/bound` proj (the bound drawn beside it), named as `shared/records.ts` names them.

## Problems

- The old `theory/` moves here. The repeated-fit study is the sweep base's hardest case: many fits per point of a curve, several knobs, the inner tapes discarded or summarised (problem 11 of `methods/README.md`); `shared/repeat.ts` is one implementation shared with `statistics` and should be the same code.
- The library gap is one file: `learn/theory.ts` (the bounds as functions, shattering and growth by enumeration, the empirical Rademacher loop, the bias-variance decomposition, effective degrees of freedom), plus the concentration bounds in `foundations/probability.ts` and James-Stein in `stats/shrinkage.ts`; the models and data exist.
- Several rows are shared with `probability` (concentration), `statistics` (shrinkage, multiple testing), `optimisation` (online learning) and `model-selection` (selection effects); each is listed where its comparison with the truth lives and the others carry `see` rows or a note.
