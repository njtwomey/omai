# Decision theory and calibration (part 4, principles of learning)

Decisions from predictions: expected loss and the Bayes decision on known class densities, the cost matrix and the threshold it implies, cost curves and cost-sensitive learning, the value of information, classifier calibration (Platt, isotonic, beta, temperature and vector scaling, histogram binning) and its evaluation, calibration for regression, conformal prediction (split, quantile, cross, Mondrian, classification sets, adaptive under shift) and uncertainty quantification (ensembles, dropout at test time, Bayesian last layers, Gaussian processes; aleatoric against epistemic), as sessions over `metrics`, `learn` (its calibrators and conformal predictors), `dists` and `stats`.

## Structure

Everything here is done to a fitted model's predictions, so it lives under `analyse/calibrate/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`): the decisions a prediction supports, the calibrators that fix its probabilities, the measures of how calibrated it is, the conformal wrappers that give it coverage, and the uncertainty estimates that give it a spread. The score slot (`shared/scores.ts`) is shared with `evaluation-metrics` (`analyse/evaluate/`), where the probabilistic metrics are measured for their own sake; the influence diagram and the value of information are `probabilistic-inference`'s (`infer/graphical/bayesian-networks/`), and the group-wise thresholds and calibration by group are `trustworthy-machine-learning`'s.

```
analyse/calibrate/
  decisions/          the Bayes decision on known densities, the cost matrix and its threshold, cost curves, cost-sensitive learning, abstention, the value of information (see probabilistic-inference)
  calibrators/        the calibrator as the mode fitted on held-out scores, before and after; multiclass calibrators; calibration inside cross-validation
  evaluation/         reliability diagrams, ECE and its binning sensitivity, the Brier decomposition, calibration over time and under shift
  regression/         calibration of a predictive distribution: PIT histograms, quantile calibration, conformal against the model's own intervals
  conformal/          split conformal intervals and sets, CQR, cross and jackknife+, Mondrian, RAPS, adaptive conformal under shift
  uncertainty/        ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, the Bayesian last layer, a Gaussian process: aleatoric against epistemic on a 1-D regression; out-of-distribution scores
  shared/             the held-out score slot, the calibrator and conformal slot tables, the reliability and coverage records (below)
```

Nothing of this chapter is built; the calibration and conformal records (`exposition/trust.ts`) and their views exist, so the first methods fill them.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. The step of a decision method is one position of its knob (a cost, a threshold); the step of a calibrator is one iteration of its fit (or one bin, or one observation for the online forms); the step of a conformal method is one arriving row or one calibration size; the step of an uncertainty method is one ensemble member or one dropout sample. Every calibration row shares `calibration/reliability` p (the reliability diagram at the viewed step, `exposition/trust.ts`: `calibrationRecord`) and `calibration/ece` s; every conformal row shares `conformal/coverage` s and `conformal/width` s (`conformalRecord`).

## Problems

- The old `trust/` calibration, conformal and uncertainty pieces move here; the measures (ECE, Brier) are shared with `evaluation-metrics` through one score slot, and the group-wise versions with `trustworthy-machine-learning`.
- The library gaps are two files: `learn/uncertainty.ts` (deep ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, disagreement) and the regression-calibration additions to `learn/calibration.ts` (quantile calibration, the PIT histogram); the `classWeight` option on the task methods' objective slot is shared with `model-selection`'s cost study.
- The uncertainty rows train networks (members, passes) and wait on the worker producer for anything beyond toy size (problem 7 of `methods/README.md`); the 1-D regression exemplar is small enough to run on the main thread.
