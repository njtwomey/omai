# Metric and similarity learning

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). pytorch-metric-learning (losses, miners, distances, reducers, regularizers, samplers, testers and the AccuracyCalculator), metric-learn (the scikit-learn-contrib learners of a Mahalanobis metric) and FAISS (the vector-index concepts: flat, IVF, product quantisation, HNSW, LSH). None is installed, so all three lists are from memory; every name was then looked up in the export list and the source of omai.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Losses: contrastive, triplet margin, InfoNCE and NT-Xent, supervised contrastive, ArcFace and CosFace, VICReg and Barlow Twins, alignment and uniformity, CLIP, plus the learning-to-rank losses (RankNet, LambdaRank, ListNet, ApproxNDCG) that a retrieval page also needs.
- Learned metrics: NCA and LMNN as stepped algorithms with the solver as a slot, so the embedding at step t is on the tape (`fittedAt(t)`); metric-learn ships them as one-shot fits.
- Retrieval and indexing: exact, k-d tree and ball-tree search under any registered distance, HNSW, IVF and LSH indexes with the graph and the buckets as traced runs, precision@k, recall@k, hit rate, MRR, NDCG, MAP and R-precision, trustworthiness and continuity.
- omai ahead: a metric learner that can be watched converge, an HNSW build that can be replayed, and distances that are a registry rather than a flag.

What is missing is concentrated in one place: everything that works on a labelled batch. pytorch-metric-learning is built around "a loss over all pairs or triplets of a batch, a miner that selects the informative ones, a sampler that makes sure the batch has positives". omai has the losses for explicit pairs and triplets and for in-batch positives, but no miners, no m-per-class sampler, and none of the multi-similarity, lifted-structure, circle, N-pairs or proxy-based losses. The catalogue plans mining (`representation-learning/triplet-and-margin`, "the mining as the mode") and the `learn/metric-learning` chapter, and neither is built. A single file of mining and a second of batch-label losses would close most of it; the proxy losses build on the class weight vectors that `arcFace` already has.

On the metric-learn side, omai has the two learners that sklearn itself ships (NCA, LMNN). The pair-constraint learners (ITML above all, then LSML, SDML, MMC) and the local Fisher and kernel-regression variants (LFDA, MLKR) are absent, as is the data shape they need (pairs, triplets and quadruplets of rows, built from labels).
