# Training loops and learning paradigms

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). The libraries that structure training and the paradigms beyond one dataset and one loss: skorch, PyTorch Lightning and Ignite (callbacks, schedulers, accumulation, early stopping), learn2learn and higher (meta-learning), Avalanche (continual learning), river (online learning and drift), Opacus (differential privacy), Flower (federated learning), modAL and small-text (active learning), Snorkel (weak supervision) and cleanlab (noisy labels, data audits). Introspected: skorch, modAL, Opacus. The rest are from memory.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Training loop: early stopping, best checkpoint, learning-rate monitors, plateau schedules, gradient accumulation and clipping, freezing, EMA and SWAG, all as pieces of one traced run (`nn/training`); the "callbacks" of Lightning and Ignite are the tape and its probes.
- Active learning: every query strategy of modAL and small-text (uncertainty, margin, entropy, query-by-committee, BALD, BatchBALD, expected error and model change, core-set, BADGE, density weighting, stream-based), with the loop as an algorithm.
- Privacy: DP-SGD with per-example clipping, RDP accounting, Gaussian, Laplace and exponential mechanisms, randomised response, membership, attribute and model-inversion attacks.
- Weak supervision and noisy labels: label models (majority, Dawid-Skene, a Snorkel-style model), noise transition estimation, confident learning (`findLabelIssues`), co-teaching, forward and backward correction, PU learning.
- Online learning: Hoeffding trees, the passive-aggressive and perceptron learners, online and kernel regressors, FTRL, bandits, drift detectors (ADWIN, KSWIN, Page-Hinkley, DDM, EDDM), streaming metrics, stream generators with concept drift.

Three paradigms with a catalogue chapter have no code at all: meta-learning (MAML, Reptile, prototypical networks and an episode sampler), continual learning (EWC, replay, LwF, a stream of tasks and the forgetting matrix) and federated learning (FedAvg, FedProx). Together with prequential evaluation, they are the clearest gaps of this family. All three are inner-loop-over-trainers programs, and `autodiff`, `rl#replayBuffer` and `optim#empiricalFisher` are what they would be built from.

`higher` is marked out of scope: its job (differentiating through an inner optimisation loop) is what `grad` of a function of functional optimiser steps already does.
