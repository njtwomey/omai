# The torch ecosystem beyond torch: architectures, audio, text, augmentation and inspection

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). torchvision.models and timm (the architecture families a learner expects), torchaudio and librosa (audio transforms and features), torchtext (vocabularies and tokenisers), kornia and albumentations (augmentation and classical vision), and the inspection side of the ecosystem (torchinfo, torch.fx, ONNX). Introspected: torchvision.models and librosa. The others are from memory. Pretrained weights, detection and video model zoos, GPU kernels and serving are out of scope on purpose, and are marked so.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Blocks: ResNet basic and bottleneck, squeeze-and-excitation, ConvNeXt, MBConv, U-Net stages, feature pyramids, transformer blocks and patch embedding, mixers, state-space layers, Mamba, RetNet, RWKV, KAN, neural operators.
- Audio: spectrograms, mel and MFCC, chroma, pitch (YIN, pYIN), onset and beat tracking, tempograms, SpecAugment, time stretching and pitch shifting, gammatone and ERB scales, masking and source separation baselines; `signal` at scipy.signal width.
- Vision (classical, OpenCV-shaped): filters, morphology, features (Harris, FAST, ORB, BRIEF, SIFT), matching, RANSAC, homography and essential matrices, stereo, calibration, bundle adjustment, optical flow, segmentation (SLIC, GrabCut, mean shift), tracking.
- Text: tokenisers (regex, treebank, tweet, BPE, WordPiece, unigram), vocabularies, n-gram models, TF-IDF, BM25, word2vec, GloVe, fastText, parsers.
- Inspection: `describe`, `summary`, layer tables, FLOP and memory estimates, receptive fields, and a scene layer that draws structures.

omai has the parts of the architectures and none of the assembled, named networks. A learner who wants "ResNet-18 at a small scale, trained on 28x28 digits and then described" has to wire it by hand; VGG-style plain stacks (the baseline for residual connections), a vision transformer and a small U-Net as builders with depth and width as knobs would close that. The augmentation side is the same: crop, flip, cutout, mixup and cutmix exist, the colour and blur modifiers, the policies (RandAugment) and the label-carrying geometric ones (boxes, keypoints, masks) do not.

## Absent and partial items

Priority is high, medium or low by how many learners would expect the thing, how many pages it unlocks and how well it shows off a stepped, traced run; not by recency. Sizes: S a day or less, M a few days, L a week or more. Homes follow `docs/structure.md`. "Nearest in omai" is what a user would reach for today. Items are grouped; the full list of names per group is in `data/match-<lib>.json`.
