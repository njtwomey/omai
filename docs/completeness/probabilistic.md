# Probabilistic programming and Bayesian workflow

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). NumPyro, PyMC, TensorFlow Probability and ArviZ cover probabilistic programs, inference engines, distributions and bijectors, and the Bayesian diagnostic workflow. torch.distributions is in torch.md. NumPyro and ArviZ were introspected; PyMC and TFP are from memory.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Programs with `sample`, `observe`, plates and effect handlers; MCMC (HMC, NUTS, Gibbs, slice, MALA, SGLD, SMC, tempered SMC), variational inference with auto-guides, Laplace, ABC, exact enumeration and conjugate engines behind one `infer`.
- Diagnostics as numbers: R-hat, ESS, MCSE, HDI, divergences, BFMI, PSIS-LOO, WAIC, model comparison and stacking weights.
- Distributions: dozens of families, compositions (mixture, truncated, censored, transformed), copulas, GAMLSS families; about 50 bijector exports including coupling and autoregressive flows.
- Every sampler is a stepped algorithm with the chain on the tape, so a posterior can be watched filling in.
