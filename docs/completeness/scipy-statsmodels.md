# scipy, statsmodels and time-series libraries

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). scipy supplies the numerical and statistical base (stats, optimize, linalg, signal, interpolate, integrate, spatial, special, sparse, fft, cluster); statsmodels the regression, GLM, test and time-series classics; sktime, tslearn and prophet the forecasting and time-series-learning layer. scipy and statsmodels were introspected; sktime, tslearn and prophet are from memory.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Statistics: about 170 test exports, power analysis, effect sizes, resampling, copulas, extreme-value fits, smoothing; the distribution families of `dists` carry log-densities, sampling and bijectors under one contract.
- Optimisation: all the scipy minimisers plus optax-style optimisers, trust regions, LP/QP/MILP, global optimisers, and every one runs as a traced Algorithm.
- Time series: ARMA/ARIMA, exponential smoothing and ETS, STL and MSTL, VAR and VECM, Johansen, GARCH family, Kalman/EKF/UKF/particle filters, matrix profile, change-point detection, shapelets.
- Numerics: dense and sparse linear algebra, Krylov solvers, quadrature, interpolation, FFT/NUFFT, polynomials, inverse problems, ODE/PDE solvers with dense output and events.
- Signal processing and LTI systems cover scipy.signal at its full width, plus audio features.
