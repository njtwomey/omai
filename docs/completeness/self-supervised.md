# Self-supervised and representation learning

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). lightly and solo-learn (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, SimSiam, DINO, SwAV, Barlow Twins, VICReg, MAE and their heads, memory banks and augmentation recipes), sentence-transformers (bi-encoders trained with in-batch negatives, evaluators), and the autoencoder, contrastive-predictive and disentanglement vocabulary of the literature. All from memory; nothing is installed.

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Objectives: InfoNCE, NT-Xent, supervised contrastive, CLIP, Barlow Twins, VICReg, alignment and uniformity, distillation, Sinkhorn, vector quantisation with its EMA codebook, the ELBO with a beta weight, the importance-weighted bound and score-matching losses.
- Representation diagnostics: linear probes, CKA, effective rank, alignment and uniformity, sparse autoencoders for activations, neighbour purity and analogy tests for word vectors.
- The catalogue plans the trainers (`representation-learning`, `generative-models`); what exists is the loss side.

The gap is the trainers: omai has the losses a self-supervised method needs and none of the assembled methods. A SimCLR or BYOL page needs a two-view augmentation, a projection head, the loss, an EMA target for BYOL and a linear probe as the evaluation; with the stepped tape, "why BYOL does not collapse" (the embedding rank at each step) is a better demonstration than the paper has. The autoencoder family (plain, denoising, sparse, VAE, VQ-VAE) is the same: losses and the quantiser exist, the model and the latent-traversal view do not.
