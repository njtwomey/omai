# Repertoire: what omai should or could have

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). The family files compare omai with named libraries. This file lists what a
learner or practitioner of ML, statistics, probabilistic modelling, RL and data science would expect from a library that
calls itself an observable ML library, whether or not one library offers it as a single API: textbook methods (Bishop,
Murphy, Hastie/Tibshirani/Friedman, Sutton and Barto, Koller and Friedman, Russell and Norvig), datasets and generators
with a known truth, diagnostics and charts, and methods that exist only as a one-shot function.

"Should have" means the catalogue plans it, or a standard course teaches it, and a learner would be surprised not to
find it. "Could have" means it would be nice and would show off the traced design, but nobody would be surprised by its
absence.

Method of checking: the methods catalogue (`docs/methods-catalog/`, 52 chapters), `docs/algorithm-gaps.md` and
`docs/aifn/future.md` were read for what is planned. Every item below was searched for by name and by concept in the
export list of omai-engine (16,750 exports) and in the engine and render source (`grep` over `packages/*/src`), after
a first pass that wrongly listed several things that exist (stochastic Dirichlet-process mixtures, determinantal point
processes, exploring starts, SMOTE, HC0 to HC3 errors, the Fashion-MNIST test set, the Adult income data). The catalogue
TODO paths (`lib/...ts`) were also checked: of the roughly 108 distinct paths all but twelve exist, and those twelve live
under other names, so the catalogue's TODO markers are stale rather than informative; `docs/methods-catalog/PLAN.md`
says the same. Sizes: S a day or less, M a few days, L a week or more.

The second pass of the survey (`README.md`, `blind-spots.md`) searched again for MAML, Reptile, prototypical networks, EWC,
experience replay for supervised streams and FedAvg in the source and the export list and found none; the chapters in the table below
are therefore still absent. Items already in `docs/algorithm-gaps.md` section (c) are marked "(C1)" and so on and are not argued again.

## What is not missing

To keep the picture honest, things the first pass flagged and the second pass found: SMOTE, borderline-SMOTE and ADASYN
(`learn/preprocessing#smote`), HC0 to HC3 standard errors (`linear#olsSummary`), the Fashion-MNIST test set and 8x8 digits,
the variational Gaussian mixture with a Dirichlet-process prior (`mixture#variationalMixtureAlgorithm`), determinantal
point processes (`stochastic/dpp.ts`), the Adult income and German credit data, Anscombe's quartet, mtcars, air passengers,
Nile, sunspots, Old Faithful, the Palmer penguins, Monte Carlo with exploring starts (`agents/tabular`), self-organising
maps (`manifold#selfOrganisingMap`), Bayesian optimisation and Hyperband in `tune`, LoRA, pruning and quantisation in
`nn`, symbolic regression and KANs, Mamba and other state-space layers, neural operators, and a self-contained set of
generative-model losses (GAN, VAE, diffusion, flow matching).

Found by the second pass of the survey, which first listed them wrongly as missing or did not expect them: linear-chain and general CRFs with
the structured perceptron and passive-aggressive training and seqeval-style entity metrics (`pgm/models/crf*.ts`,
`metrics/sequence-labelling.ts`), ProbLog-style probabilistic logic programs with EM (`logic/problog.ts`), queueing theory,
Gillespie's algorithm, the Polya urn and Markov-chain mixing times (`stochastic`), 3-D Gaussian splatting and PINN losses (`fields`,
`losses/physics.ts`), the MINE bound (`information#mineBound`), DP-SGD with RDP accounting, a Snorkel-style label model, confident
learning, drift detectors (ADWIN, KSWIN, DDM, EDDM, Page-Hinkley), a Hoeffding tree, every query strategy of modAL and small-text,
explainable boosting machines, CORELS, the HNSW, IVF and LSH indexes, the algebraic Riccati solvers (`numerics/linalg#solveCare`) and
nearly all of stumpy and python-control.
