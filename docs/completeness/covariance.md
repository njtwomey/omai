# Covariance, precision and correlation estimation

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). A survey of covariance, precision and correlation estimation and what sits next to it, added in a third pass because the first two saw no gap: `sklearn.covariance` is fully covered (all 16 names), and that is the only lens the first pass had on the subject. This file widens it to the libraries where the subject lives: scikit-learn and statsmodels again (a slice, re-listed), pyRiemann, geomstats and PyManopt (SPD matrices), skfolio, PyPortfolioOpt and Riskfolio-Lib (risk models), skggm, gglasso and regain (graphical models), robpy (robust scatter), rmt_denoise, arch (long-run covariance), nilearn and MNE (connectomes and noise covariance), the R packages that have no Python peer (the CRAN reference-manual indexes of CovTools, rrcov, robustbase, cellWise, corpcor, huge, nlshrink, POET, rags2ridges and BDgraph) and concept lists for the parts of the literature no package covers whole (sparse and structured estimators, graph-structure methods, covariance tests, random matrix theory, missing data and streaming, time-varying and high-frequency covariance, SPD geometry). Readings: sklearn, statsmodels and scipy were introspected from the installed packages; pyriemann, skfolio, PyPortfolioOpt, Riskfolio-Lib, skggm, gglasso, regain, robpy, rmt_denoise, geomstats, pymanopt, arch, nilearn and MNE were not installed and not imported: each was downloaded as a wheel (an sdist for skggm) into a scratch directory and read with `ast` by `enumerate.py` (the `OMAI_SURVEY_SRC` route), so the names are the release ones (`ref-*.json` records the version). The CRAN lists are the index pages of the reference manuals; the pandas, river, JavaScript, PyMC and the concept lists are from memory and are checklists, not inventories. `skggm` has no wheel and its sdist was read; `arch` has no multivariate GARCH, so only its kernel long-run covariance module is listed.

## What omai has today

Read from `packages/engine/src/lib` (the exports and the source, not the index alone). Covariance estimation lives in one folder and is spread
over about ten others; nothing below is new, it is the inventory the verdicts were made against.

Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- The sklearn.covariance estimators: empirical, shrunk, Ledoit-Wolf, OAS, FastMCD (with the concentration steps traced) and the elliptic envelope, and the graphical lasso by coordinate descent with the primal-dual gap, the objective and the edge count on the tape and the path of partial correlations as an exposition.
- The pieces around them that a covariance estimator is built from: weighted `cov` and `corrcoef`, partial correlation, distance correlation and its test, a Kendall tau matrix, factor analysis, probabilistic PCA and sparse PCA (the low-rank-plus-diagonal covariance), the Wishart, inverse Wishart, LKJ and matrix-normal distributions with the inverse-Wishart conjugate update, Box M, Hotelling, MANOVA, Mardia and Little tests, `sqrtm`, `logm`, `expm`, `cholesky` with rank-one updates, Toeplitz and circulant matrices, shrinkage discriminant analysis and the Gaussian mixture covariance types.
- Mahalanobis in every place it is used (distances registry, k-NN, matching, elliptic envelope), covariance ellipses as data, a correlation chart in the renderer and a streaming regularised covariance inside the sampler's mass-matrix adaptation.
- Every iterative estimator already runs as a stepped algorithm; the survey finds nothing that is a one-shot function where a library would have an iteration.

Reading the counts: the re-listed slice (sklearn.covariance, a slice of statsmodels and of scipy) repeats names the first pass already counted and is left out of the family totals in the README. The remaining libraries add the names below. Concept lists (the last seven tables) are lists of methods, not of API names, so their absent counts say how much of the literature is not in omai, not how many functions are missing. Two first-pass verdicts were corrected here and in their own files: `GraphicalLassoCV` is partial (omai has no estimator that chooses the penalty by cross-validation, EBIC or stability, only the path as an exposition), and `statsmodels.stats.api.test_cov*` were counted partial by a group rule and are absent (the one-sample tests of a covariance matrix) except `test_cov_oneway`, which is Box M.

`sklearn_covariance`, `statsmodels_cov`, `scipy_cov` repeat names the first pass already counted (in sklearn.md, scipy-statsmodels.md); their rows are in the tables but not in the totals of `counts.json` (they are under `relisted`) and the README.

## What to add: ranked, in batches one agent could take

Directional, by the survey's rule: how many users expect the thing, how well a stepped, traced version shows what omai is for, and how cheap it is.
Sizes: S a day or less, M a few days, L a week or more. Homes are proposals under `packages/engine/src/lib` and follow the shape of the folder
(`covariance/` gains files; `docs/structure.md` section 3 and `make barrels` need an entry when one is built). Everything is a library module with tests, TSDoc and an
`@index` entry; nothing here is for the gallery except where marked "new render capability", and a poc only when the owner asks. Names are checked, behaviour is not.

### Where a stepped, traced version teaches something a one-shot function cannot

- The Marchenko-Pastur eigenvalue plot with denoising, and the BBP transition (gap 1): a spike is invisible below `1 + sqrt(q)` and escapes the bulk above it.
- The graphical-lasso path with the stepped solver (gap 2): the sweep, the duality gap, the edges entering as the penalty falls, and the choice of the penalty by three rules.
- Riemannian geometry of SPD matrices (gap 7): the geodesic between two ellipses and the Karcher mean converging step by step.
- Shrinkage intensity as a slider (gap 4): the eigenvalue spectrum, the condition number and the held-out likelihood against the intensity.
- Nearest correlation matrix by Dykstra, the Tyler fixed point, the flip-flop for a matrix-normal and the EM covariance with missing values: four short iterations whose convergence is the figure.
- Principal component pursuit (later): a video-like matrix splitting into low rank and sparse parts as the ADMM iterates.
- Time-varying graphical lasso and DCC correlation (later): a graph or a correlation that moves.
- New render capabilities these would need, to be checked against the existing recipes before a recipe is added: an overlay of a density on a histogram (the eigenvalues), a correlation matrix in clustered order with the dendrogram, and a cell map (a heatmap of flagged cells for cellwise robustness).

### What this survey could not see

- Behaviour. All verdicts are by name; an omai function can be thinner than its namesake (the graphical lasso has the algorithm of sklearn but not its `GraphicalLassoCV`; the 16 of sklearn.covariance were counted from names, and one of them, `GraphicalLassoCV`, did not survive a second look).
- The R packages were read as index pages, not as source, and robustbase only to the first 100,000 characters of its index. Several R packages with a Python peer that is not installable (for instance `mgarch`-style DCC packages) were not looked at; `arch` was read and has no multivariate model.
- skggm has no wheel and was read from its sdist; it is not maintained. gglasso, regain, robpy and rmt_denoise are small research packages, so a gap against one of them is a gap against a method, not against a standard.
- The concept lists (sparse and structured, graph methods, tests, random matrix theory, missing data, time-varying, SPD concepts, and the pandas, river, JavaScript and PyMC lists) were written from memory. They list methods a reader would recognise; they are not inventories of a release, and a short list means the author remembered little, not that little exists.
- Nothing about numerical agreement: omai's Ledoit-Wolf, OAS, FastMCD and graphical lasso have golden tests against scikit-learn in the repository, and that is the only agreement evidence this survey relies on.
