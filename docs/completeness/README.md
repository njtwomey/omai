# Completeness survey: omai against the libraries closest to it

Where omai is thinner than the established libraries it resembles, and what to add next so that a learner or practitioner
finds what they expect. omai is a teaching and exploration library, so the question is not "is it state of the art" but
"would a reasonable user look for this, how many users is that, and would a stepped, traced version of it show off what
omai is for". GPU kernels, distributed training, production serving, large pretrained models and AutoML platforms are left
out on purpose.

This is a quick survey, accurate at the level of names. Nothing here compares behaviour or numerical agreement.

The survey ran in three passes. The first pass measured omai against the libraries of the domains the owner listed (scikit-learn, scipy,
statsmodels, torch, probabilistic programming, graphical models, RL, graph learning and the like). The owner's reaction was that
families such as metric learning were pretty much absent from it, and that was right: a survey only measures the libraries it lists.
The second pass added the families the first one left out (metric learning, self-supervised learning, training loops and paradigms,
Bayesian optimisation and Gaussian-process stacks, tabular and interpretable libraries, the torch ecosystem, structured prediction
and CRFs, and a group of adjacent libraries) and then asked which omai domains had no reference lens at all
([blind-spots.md](blind-spots.md)). The third pass took one area on its own: covariance, precision and correlation estimation
([covariance.md](covariance.md)), which the first two had seen only through `sklearn.covariance` (16 names, all covered) and so reported as complete. Measured
against the libraries where the subject lives, it is not: that is where the largest absences of omai's strongest estimator family are.

Start with [plan.md](plan.md): the short, directional version (the big gaps, an order, and what needs a decision).

What the second-pass numbers say. The counts here are higher in "absent or partial" than the first pass partly because these are
the families omai was less likely to have, and partly because many lists are long tails (every loss and every head of a
self-supervised library, every test problem of pymoo, every game of OpenSpiel). Read the "Bringing omai to the next level" table, not
the counts. Concretely:
- Metric learning: omai has the losses for explicit pairs and triplets, NCA and LMNN with the solver as a slot, and the retrieval indexes (HNSW, IVF, LSH) and metrics;
  it has none of the batch-label layer (miners, an m-per-class sampler, multi-similarity, lifted-structure, circle, N-pairs and proxy losses) and only two of the
  metric-learn learners (ITML and the other pair-constraint learners are absent). All 114 names of pytorch-metric-learning are in `metric-learning.md` with their verdict.
- Self-supervised learning: the objectives are there, the assembled methods (SimCLR, BYOL, MoCo, DINO, MAE) and the autoencoder models are not.
- Training and paradigms: training-loop pieces, active learning, privacy, weak supervision and noisy labels, drift and streams are strong. Meta-learning, continual learning and federated
  learning have no code, nor does prequential evaluation.
- Bayesian optimisation and GPs: the single-point acquisitions and exact and sparse GPs are there; batch, entropy-search and multi-objective acquisitions, multi-output and variational GPs
  are not. Pyro's handlers and guides are mostly present, the reparametrisation handlers and enumerated ELBO are not.
- Tabular and interpretable: EBMs, rule lists, RuleFit, CORELS, stacking, feature selection and the diagnostic charts are there; categorical encoders (WoE, ordered target statistics),
  monotone constraints, FIGS and hierarchical shrinkage, multi-label learners and a few charts are not.
- Torch ecosystem: the blocks and the audio, vision and text pipelines are strong; the assembled named networks (ResNet, VGG, ViT) and the augmentation policies are not.
- Structured prediction: CRFs are a strength and, as of 0.1.12, so is the ring around them (neural CRF layer and BiLSTM tagger, constrained and n-best decoding, structured SVMs, MEMM, semi-Markov, latent and partial-label CRFs, graph CRFs, graph-cut and AD3 MAP), all in `pgm/structured` (see `structured-prediction.md` for what is still missing).
- Adjacent: control, filtering, stumpy, optax, game theory and conformal prediction are close to complete; data-audit reports, conformal risk control, forecasting as a pipeline,
  neighbourhood and graph collaborative filtering, the classic board-game shelf and multi-objective evolutionary algorithms are the gaps.

What the third pass says. The count is high because 157 of the 737 absences are names of the SPD-geometry libraries (pyRiemann, geomstats, PyManopt) and 213 are entries of the concept
lists of methods; read the ranked list in `covariance.md`, not the count. In short: omai has the sklearn.covariance estimators and a traced graphical lasso, and nothing around them. Missing are
the random-matrix toolbox (Marchenko-Pastur, denoising and detoning, nonlinear shrinkage), the choice of the graphical-lasso penalty (cross-validation, EBIC, StARS), shrinkage targets
other than a scaled identity, nearest-correlation repair, HAC and cluster-robust covariance for regression, multivariate running and exponentially weighted covariance, the geometry of SPD
matrices, robust scatter beyond MCD (Tyler, M-estimators, OGK), the one-sample covariance and sphericity tests, and nonparanormal and neighbourhood graph estimators. The multivariate
Student t distribution is absent from `dists`. Ten of the R packages practitioners expect have no maintained Python peer (corpcor, CovTools, nlshrink, POET, rags2ridges, BDgraph,
spcov, covglasso, rmgarch, highfrequency); robpy, skggm, gglasso and regain are small and partly unmaintained, and no JavaScript library goes past `cov` and `corrcoef` of two arrays.

How the counts were made, and how far to trust them:
- Introspected names were matched against the 16,750 exports of omai-engine as `scripts/aifn-usage/scan-omai.ts` reads them
  (the repository's own docs extractor). Exact name after dropping case and underscores counts as covered
  (`LogisticRegression` and `logisticRegression`, `rgb2hsv` and `rgbToHsv`). Everything else was judged by hand, with
  the verdict and the nearest omai thing kept in `scripts/completeness-survey/verdicts/*.txt` (one rule per line, a
  regular expression over reference names, so one line can settle many names).
- A name match can be a false friend (same name, different thing), and a hand-written family rule can be too generous.
  Several families (torchmetrics, SHAP, Captum, Optuna, the scikit-image and NLTK areas marked "covered") were settled by
  reading omai's index and domain summaries and then matching whole groups of names, not one by one. Where a rule says
  "the part to check", the group is probably complete but a few members may be missing.
- The absences behind the 25 additions above and the items of `repertoire.md` were searched for again in the source tree and
  the index (a second pass found and removed several wrong claims: SMOTE, HC0 to HC3 standard errors, Fashion-MNIST, Dirichlet-process mixtures,
  Adult and German credit data, determinantal point processes were all already there). The other absences got the
  automatic check only (`match.ts --recheck`: candidate omai names sharing word pieces), which can miss a thing
  that omai names differently.
- "Partial" is a judgement, not a measurement. Several partials are "the algorithm exists but there is no estimator
  wrapper" or "one-d only".
- The prophet list is method names of one class, so its counts are not meaningful.
- Second pass: the same machinery (`enumerate.py`, `match.ts`, one verdict file per family under `verdicts/`). For metric learning, the CRF
  libraries and the CRF-related items, every name was checked individually in the export list and the source. For the rest, names
  were matched automatically, the unmatched ones reviewed by hand, and the absent or partial claims of most families
  were run through `match.ts --recheck` (candidate omai names sharing word pieces), which turned up and corrected several claims
  (ProbLog, MINE, the Riccati solvers, queueing theory, winsorising, the LPC and UMAP utilities, Bayesian linear regression).
  Search of the source (`grep`) backed the absences behind the new "next level" items.
- Lists from memory (59 of the 91) are checklists, not inventories. Large libraries (SymPy, POT, cvxpy atoms, the pymoo problem suites,
  the OpenSpiel game list) were judged by group; the verdict file says which groups.
- An omai export that has the right name but is a different thing is a false friend (`nn#marginLoss` is the Carlini-Wagner margin, not
  pytorch-metric-learning's MarginLoss); several were found and the verdict says so.
- Third pass (covariance): `enumerate.py` gained a source route: libraries that are not installed are downloaded as wheels (an sdist for skggm) into a scratch directory with
  `pip download --no-deps`, unpacked, and read with `ast` through `OMAI_SURVEY_SRC` (nothing is imported or installed, and the project's dependency groups are untouched); the
  names are the release's. The CRAN lists are the index pages of the reference manuals; the other lists and the concept lists are from memory (`memory.json`). Every name has a hand-written
  verdict in `verdicts/covariance.txt` (none was left to the name match). Hand-written prose of that family is in `scripts/completeness-survey/prose/` and is included by `report.ts`.

in `report.ts` and rerun. The enumeration here was run with the interpreter of the repository's existing oracle virtualenv. `enumerate.py` needs the libraries to be installed in a Python environment (the repository's
oracle groups, or any environment that has them).

## Blind spots: what the survey could not see

A survey that lists libraries can only describe the domains the libraries touch. The second pass mapped all 67 folders of
`packages/engine/src/lib` and the areas of `omai-render` against the libraries of both passes (`blind-spots.md` has the table).
- **No lens at all:** `base`, `context`, `exposition`, `scene` and `trace` (omai's own infrastructure and tape, nothing to compare) and
  `fields`, `foundations`, `graphics`, `problems`, `search`, `worlds` and the non-ODE half of `dynamics` (PDEs, automata, celestial mechanics, fractals).
- **A lens too thin to call a measurement:** `logic` (ProbLog only; the SAT, ASP and planning libraries of the environment were not enumerated),
  `stochastic` (SDE solvers only), `rules`, `survival`, `gam`, `kernel-methods`, `fairness`, `anomaly` and `agents` (lists from memory).
- **Quick textbook passes** (names looked up in the export list and the source) for search and planning, logic, stochastic processes,
  information theory, fields and graphics, dynamics, foundations and text: omai is deep in all of them. Gaps found: SMA*, AND-OR search,
  online search, RRT and D* Lite; BDDs, SMT, CTL and LTL model checking; Jackson networks and coupling from the past; total
  correlation, the variation of information and partial information decomposition; marching cubes, radiosity, ambient occlusion,
  tone mapping and subdivision surfaces; delay differential equations, Lambert's problem, incompressible Navier-Stokes, molecular dynamics and neuron models;
  POS taggers, Lesk, VADER, language identification and doc2vec.
- **What a name comparison cannot see:** behaviour and numerical agreement, depth of options, composition (a capability offered as a
  configuration), teaching quality and examples, interactivity and traces (the thing omai is for, which no library offers), scale and
  robustness, ecosystem integration, anything no library lists, and, for the 59 lists written from memory, anything the author did not remember.
  The survey will never be complete; a short gap list for a domain means the lens showed little, not that nothing is missing.

## Bringing omai to the next level

## Deliberately not worth adding

- Anything that needs a GPU, a cluster or a server: `DataParallel`, `SyncBatchNorm`, `torch.compile`, subprocess vector
  environments, Optuna storages (RDB, Redis, gRPC), Ray.
- Large pretrained models and their loaders (Hugging Face, torchvision model zoo, NLTK corpora and Stanford wrappers,
  spaCy), and anything that downloads data at run time.
- Physics engines for 3-D MuJoCo bodies (Humanoid, Ant) and Box2D walkers and pixel racing, Atari emulation.
- File formats of other ecosystems with no teaching value: MATLAB, NetCDF, Fortran, Harwell-Boeing, xarray/InferenceData
  converters, HTML and JavaScript export of SHAP, pickle.
- The long tail of scipy.special (Mathieu, spheroidal, Kelvin functions, zeros of Bessel functions), LAPACK helper
  wrappers, and the 100 or so rarely used scipy.stats distributions that are reparametrisations of ones omai has.
- Mixins, tags, parameter validation, warnings, `check_array`-style validation, and metadata routing: omai has typed
  `Space`s and its own contracts.
- Pretrained backbones and zoos behind self-supervised, sentence-embedding, speech and detection libraries; experiment trackers,
  loggers, checkpoints on disk, cluster launchers and distributed strategies behind Lightning, Ignite and Ray; GPU-only indexes
  and approximate-search engines at billion scale (FAISS beyond the concepts); export to ONNX for serving.
- AutoML search over pipelines, and the Brill/Hunpos/Senna taggers and similar wrappers around external tools.
- Quantum computing, population-based training and the other items of
  `docs/algorithm-gaps.md` section (c) are separate decisions: they are absences, but they are not what a learner of the
  libraries above would look for first.
