# Graph neural networks and graph libraries

Part of the [completeness survey](README.md). PyTorch Geometric (and DGL, which has the same scope) for GNN layers, pooling, batching, samplers, transforms and datasets; NetworkX for classical graph algorithms, since omai's `graph` domain is NetworkX-shaped. NetworkX was introspected; PyG is from memory (it is not installed).

Counts are of names: "covered" means an omai export or a documented configuration does what the reference name does, "partial" means a different shape or fewer options, "absent" means nothing found by name or by concept, and "out of scope" is plumbing, GPU or production features, downloads and the like that omai should not copy. Semantics were not compared.

## Where omai is already strong

- Layers: GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GIN, relational GCN, EGNN and a graph transformer, on a general message-passing function; spectral filters (Chebyshev, heat kernel, wavelets).
- Sampling: neighbour sampling with variance estimates, layer-wise importance sampling, random-walk subgraphs, node2vec walks; positional encodings (Laplacian, random walk); Weisfeiler-Leman.
- Classical algorithms: centralities, community detection (Louvain, Leiden, Girvan-Newman, label propagation), flows and cuts, matching, shortest paths, spanning trees, isomorphism (VF2), percolation, epidemics on networks.
- Everything above that is iterative runs as a traced algorithm.

## Absent and partial items

Priority is high, medium or low by how many learners would expect the thing, how many pages it unlocks and how well it shows off a stepped, traced run; not by recency. Sizes: S a day or less, M a few days, L a week or more. Homes follow `docs/structure.md`. "Nearest in omai" is what a user would reach for today. Items are grouped; the full list of names per group is in `data/match-<lib>.json`.
