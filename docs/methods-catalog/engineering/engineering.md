# Machine learning engineering (part 8, practice)

Training and serving mechanics at small scale: training at scale (gradient accumulation, data, model and pipeline parallelism simulated on a tiny model, mixed precision's effect on a loss, checkpointing and resumption), efficient models (quantisation, pruning and the lottery ticket, distillation, low-rank compression, fast decoding, the cost model of an architecture), serving (batching and latency as a queue, canary and shadow deployment), monitoring (streaming metrics, drift by reference), the data pipeline (loading, sampling and bucketing) and reproducibility (keys and determinism), as sessions over `nn` (training, quantise, pruning, describe, decoding), `optim`, `losses`, `stochastic` (queues), `data` (batching, samplers, loaders), `stats` (sequential tests, running statistics), `random` and `trace`. The distributed and hardware parts are simulated (a cluster of workers with clocks and latencies on one thread), which every such page says.

## Structure

Everything here is a study over a training or a serving process: the same model trained two ways (accumulated against batched, pruned against dense, private against not), or a process simulated and measured (workers, queues, streams); nothing is a model of its own. So it lives under `study/engineering/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`), one folder per concern. The alternative, the serving and cluster simulations under `simulate/` beside the queues of `stochastic`, is recorded under Problems; they stay here because their question (latency against throughput for a model) is this chapter's and the trainer they drive is shared.

```
study/engineering/
  training/             gradient accumulation, data parallelism simulated, model and pipeline parallelism simulated, mixed precision, checkpointing and resumption
  efficiency/           quantisation with the method as a mode, pruning with the schedule as a mode, distillation, low-rank compression, fast decoding, the cost model
  serving/              batching and latency as a queue, canary and shadow deployment
  monitoring/           streaming metrics with delayed labels; drift by reference
  pipeline/             loading, sampling and bucketing; reproducibility by keys
  shared/               the simulated cluster, the request stream, the memory and compute accounting records (below)
```

Nothing of this chapter is built; it waits on the trainer base of `methods/shared/bases/` (section 4) for every training row.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. The step of a training study is one optimiser update (or one micro-batch where that is the point); the step of a simulated cluster is one simulated clock tick; the step of a compression study is one bit width, one pruning round or one rank; the step of a queue is one arrival or departure; the step of a monitor is one batch of a stream. Every training row logs `training/loss`, the held-out metric at a schedule (`eval/<metric>`) and `model/params`, so two runs compare on one cursor (section 5 of `methods/README.md`, the comparison panel).

## Problems

- The library gap is small: float16 emulation in `floating-point.ts`, GPTQ's columns as steps; the rest is the chapter's own simulation (`shared/cluster.ts`, `shared/requests.ts`), which is not library code and says so.
- The simulated and the real (section 6, problem 16): parallelism, precision below float32 and serving are simulated on one thread; the mechanism is the point, and no page claims a measurement of hardware.
- `serving/` and the cluster could live under `simulate/` beside the queues; they stay here because the trainer and the cost model they drive are this chapter's. Decide when the first is built.
- The comparison of two runs on one cursor (section 6, problem 4) is the chapter's main render need: nearly every row here is "against the other way".
