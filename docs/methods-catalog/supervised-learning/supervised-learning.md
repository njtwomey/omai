# Supervised learning (part 5, learning paradigms)

The supervised models, each generic in its slots: regression (least squares, ridge, lasso, elastic net, robust, quantile, polynomial and spline bases), classification (logistic, linear and quadratic discriminants, perceptron), generalised linear models, generative classifiers (naive Bayes), nearest neighbours, kernel methods and support vector machines, Gaussian processes, trees and ensembles, generalised additive models, multilabel and multi-output wrappers, structured prediction, ordinal regression and survival learning, as sessions over `linear`, `kernels`, `optim` (the gradient solvers), `learn`, `losses`, `stats/smoothing` and the tree, ensemble, SVM and GP estimators moving to `lib`. A linear model is one method whose loss, penalty, solver and basis are slots (section 7.1 of `methods/README.md`); a tree family is one method per distinct grower; an ensemble is one method per kind with its base estimator as a slot.

## Structure

Everything here fits a model to labelled rows, so it lives under `fit/classify/`, `fit/regress/` and `fit/survive/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`), one folder per model family. A family that serves both tasks (linear, neighbours, kernel, Gaussian process, trees) has a folder on each side; the grower, the dual solver or the posterior is written once (its library algorithm) and the second folder's table says what the task changes (the criterion, the likelihood, the loss). The alternative, one `fit/supervised/` root with the kind as a knob, is recorded under Problems.

```
fit/classify/
  linear/             the linear classifier: loss × penalty × solver × basis (built: logistic regression by IRLS); the perceptron and the linear SVM are its loss modes
  discriminant/       linear, quadratic, regularised and Fisher's discriminant; LDA as a transformer
  naive-bayes/        Gaussian, multinomial, Bernoulli, categorical and complement naive Bayes; tree-augmented naive Bayes and AODE
  neighbours/         k-nearest and radius neighbours, nearest and shrunken centroids, metric learning (NCA, LMNN)
  kernel/             support vector machines by SMO, kernel logistic regression, the kernel Fisher discriminant, random features
  gaussian-process/   GP classification by the Laplace and EP approximations
  trees/              CART grown split by split, ID3, C4.5 and CHAID, cost-complexity pruning
  ensembles/          bagging, random forests and extra trees, AdaBoost, gradient and histogram boosting, voting and stacking
  multiclass/         one-versus-rest, one-versus-one, output codes, classifier chains and label powerset as slots over any base
  ordinal/            cumulative-link, continuation-ratio and adjacent-category models
  structured/         the structured perceptron; linear-chain CRFs are `infer/graphical/chains/`
fit/regress/
  linear/             the linear model: loss × penalty × solver × basis (built: ridge, the lasso path, conjugate Bayesian regression); the regularisation path as a study
  glm/                every exponential family with its link, IRLS stepped; zero-inflated and hurdle models; mixed models
  robust/             RANSAC, Theil-Sen and the M-estimators' row weights (Huber and quantile are loss modes of `linear/`)
  additive/           GAMs with backfitting and penalised IRLS as modes, smoothness selection, tensor-product smooths; GAMLSS
  kernel/             kernel ridge, support vector regression, random-feature regression
  gaussian-process/   the prior and the posterior, marginal-likelihood fitting, sparse and multi-output GPs, the relevance vector machine
  trees/              regression trees and their ensembles: the growers of `fit/classify/` with the regression criteria and losses
  neighbours/         k-nearest regression and the kernel smoothers (Nadaraya-Watson, local linear, LOWESS)
  experts/            the mixture of linear experts (built), hierarchical experts, switching regression
  multi-output/       multi-output regressors and regressor chains
fit/survive/          Kaplan-Meier and Nelson-Aalen, Cox proportional hazards, accelerated failure time, time-dependent scores
shared/               the chapter's slot tables (kernel, basis, base estimator), the labelled data slot, the probes of classifiers and regressors (below)
```

The built `methods/linear/logisticRegression.ts` moves to `fit/classify/linear/`, `methods/linear/ridge.ts`, `lassoPath.ts` and `bayesianRegression.ts` to `fit/regress/linear/`, and `methods/mixture/moe.ts` to `fit/regress/experts/`. `methods/vision/haarBoosting.ts` stays in `process/images/` as the Haar instance of AdaBoost; the generic AdaBoost over any base is `fit/classify/ensembles/`.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. Every method on the iterative base logs `fit/iteration/end` e, the solver's channels as `solver/...`, `model/params` p and the probes of its kind (`model/decision-surface` p and `model/predictions` p for classifiers and regressors), so the tables name only what a method adds. The status is `built <path>` (with the modes built), `partly` or empty; library readiness is in the needs column (a function named there exists; `TODO:` names what is missing and its proposed home). The step of a fit is an iteration of its solver; a closed-form fit runs as a one-step algorithm so that it has a tape like any other.

## Problems

- The biggest chapter, and the slot design is proved here first: logistic regression with every mode is the first exemplar of section 8, and nothing else in these tables moves before it holds.
- Estimators against methods: the built rows are `learn` estimators on the iterative base, made into methods by the host; the fold of the two contracts (problem 3 of `methods/README.md`) is decided on this chapter.
- Trees and ensembles are the largest library gap: `lib/trees` (the structure, the criteria, the grower as a stepped `Algorithm`, pruning) and `lib/ensembles` (bagging, forests, the generic AdaBoost, gradient and histogram boosting, stacking). Two whole-model functions exist in `explain` and should move under them and be re-exported: the CART of `explain/surrogates.ts` (`surrogateTree`, with `TreeModel` and `treePredict` in `explain/shapley.ts`) and the cyclic booster of `explain/glassbox.ts` (`explainableBoosting`). The AdaBoost of `vision/haar.ts` takes Haar windows, not a base estimator; the generic form should take any base, with the Haar instance a special case.
- Classification and regression twins of one algorithm (trees, neighbours, kernel, Gaussian process, linear) live in two folders, the regression table pointing at the classification twin where the grower or solver is the same. The alternative is one `fit/supervised/` root with the kind decided by the data; recorded here, decided when the tree grower is built.
- `fit/survive/` has its section in the layout but almost nothing in the library (the generator `survivalData` and `censoredNll`); the three rows above are one `lib/survival/` away.
- `inference-and-diagnostics` belongs to `statistics` (`analyse/statistics/`); the rows here use its summary as a probe.
- Moves: `methods/linear/` to `fit/classify/linear/` and `fit/regress/linear/`, `methods/mixture/moe.ts` to `fit/regress/experts/`; `methods/vision/haarBoosting.ts` stays in `process/images/`.
