# Recommendation and retrieval (part 7, application domains)

Recommenders and retrieval models: collaborative filtering (popularity and neighbourhood methods, matrix factorisation by SGD and ALS, implicit feedback), feature-interaction models (factorisation machines and their deep forms), neural, sequential and graph recommenders at toy scale, cold start and content, the loss families (pointwise, pairwise, listwise) and learning to rank, unbiased learning to rank from clicks (position bias, exposure), feedback loops, evaluation with beyond-accuracy objectives (diversity, novelty, coverage), retrieval models (TF-IDF and BM25, probabilistic retrieval, dense retrieval with approximate nearest neighbours), near-duplicate detection, fuzzy matching and record linkage, as sessions over `datasets` (ratings, click logs, MovieLens), `losses` (ranking, contrastive, sampled softmax), `metrics` (ranking), `numerics/spatial` (indexes), `text` (vectorisers, shingles, fingerprints, approximate search), `nn`, `rl` (off-policy estimators) and `agents` (bandits).

## Structure

Ranking is a task, so everything here lives under `fit/rank/` (section 3 of `methods/README.md`): the recommenders and the learning-to-rank models are fitted to interactions and scored by ranking metrics; the retrieval models score a query against a corpus and are fitted (a dual encoder) or built (an index) the same way. The indexes and the matching methods are operations on a corpus watched rather than models fitted; they stay here because they share the query probe and the ranking metrics, and the alternative (`process/text/retrieval/`) is recorded under Problems. The bandits that recommend are `online-experimentation`'s agents on a slate; fair ranking is `trustworthy-machine-learning`'s.

```
fit/rank/
  recommend/            popularity and neighbours, matrix factorisation with the solver as a mode, factorisation machines, neural, two-tower, sequential and graph recommenders, cold start, bandit recommenders, feedback loops
  learning-to-rank/     the ranking loss as a slot on one scorer, unbiased learning to rank from clicks
  retrieval/            lexical retrieval with the scorer as a mode, dense and hybrid retrieval with re-ranking
  indexes/              approximate nearest neighbours with the index as a mode (trees, LSH, IVF and PQ, HNSW, NN-descent)
  matching/             near-duplicates by fingerprints, fuzzy matching, record linkage
  evaluation/           offline protocols and the ranking metrics, beyond-accuracy objectives, interleaving
  shared/               the interaction and query slots, the scorer contract, the ranking probes and the feedback-loop simulator (below)
```

Nothing of this chapter is built.

## Methods

Tags on tracks: `s` a per-step scalar, `t` a per-step tensor at summary detail, `t!` a tensor kept in full, `p` a probe computed from the state at any step, `proj` a projection over the tape, `e` an event, `m` a message with a payload. The step of a fit is one epoch or one solver iteration; the step of an index is one point inserted or one query; the step of a feedback loop is one round of recommendations and responses. Every ranking method answers the same probe, `model/ranking` (the ranked list for a chosen user or query with scores and the truth's relevance), and logs the same held-out metrics (`eval/ndcg`, `eval/precisionAtK`, `eval/recallAtK`, `eval/coverage`) at a schedule.

## Problems

- The library gap is the matrix-completion solvers (`lib/numerics/linalg/completion.ts`: SGD with biases, ALS, implicit ALS, NMF, soft-impute as stepped algorithms), the recommender modules (`lib/nn/blocks/recommender.ts`: FM and its deep forms), the click models (`lib/learn/click-models.ts`), the inverted index and the probabilistic scorers (`lib/text/retrieval.ts`), and record linkage (`lib/text/linkage.ts`); everything else is in place.
- `indexes/` and `matching/` are operations on a corpus, not fits; `process/text/retrieval/` is the alternative home, and costs nothing to adopt later because both share `shared/queries.ts` and the query probe.
- Toy scale: the neural, sequential and graph recommenders show a mechanism on generated interactions; the pages say so, and the comparison that matters is with matrix factorisation on the same data.
- The MovieLens sample is a remote asset with research terms; a page that uses it says so, and the generators carry the truths the real data lacks.
