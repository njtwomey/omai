# Survey of probabilistic programming systems, and what omai takes from each

Part of the [probabilistic programming plan](PLAN.md). This is the field survey (section 3 of the plan), kept in its own
file because it is long.

### Verified online (2026-11-10)

Full list with sources: verification.md section 8. The facts the survey now relies on:
- **Pyro 1.9.2** was released 2026-10-03 (after 1.9.1 on 2024-06-02): active but slow. Its ELBOs include `TraceGraph_ELBO`,
  `TraceEnum_ELBO`, `TraceTailAdaptive_ELBO`, `TraceTMC_ELBO`, `ReweightedWakeSleep`; its autoguides include `AutoStructured`,
  `AutoGaussian` (sparse precision from the model graph) and messenger-based guides; `poutine` has `escape`, `equalize`, `lift`,
  `collapse` (experimental, needs funsor), `reparam`.
- **PyMC 6.4.0** (2026-11-01) with **pymc-extras 0.16.0**: `pm.sample` (NUTS, HMC, Metropolis family, Slice, `CompoundStep`),
  `pm.sample_smc`, `pm.fit` (ADVI, full-rank ADVI, SVGD, ASVGD), `pm.do`, `pm.observe`, and in pymc-extras `marginalize`,
  `fit_laplace`, `fit_pathfinder`, `fit_dadvi`, `fit_INLA`.
- **CmdStan 2.40** (2026-09-16; 2.39 on 2026-05-19 added the embedded Laplace approximation); methods sample, optimize,
  pathfinder, variational (ADVI, still labelled experimental), laplace, `log_prob`, `generate_quantities`, diagnose.
- **Infer.NET** is open source (MIT, not archived), last NuGet release 0.4.2504.701 (2025-04-07); algorithms EP (default), VMP
  and Gibbs; message operators are named `<Recipient>AverageConditional` (EP, Gibbs) and `<Recipient>AverageLogarithm` (VMP).
- **TFP** 0.25.0 is from 2024-11-08; no official maintenance statement; nightlies continue. **Bean Machine** is archived; **Figaro,
  WebPPL, Anglican** are dormant; **Gen.jl 0.4.9, Turing.jl 0.49.0, NIMBLE 1.4.3, Birch 2.1.6, JAGS 5.0.1** are maintained.

## What the survey says, in six sentences

1. The common core of the field is a program or graph, a log density on unconstrained space, and a menu of engines; omai has
   this and its menu is long (inventory). The differences that matter are in how the user controls the menu.
2. The systems people call "powerful" for control are NIMBLE (edit the sampler table), Turing.jl and Bean Machine
   (compose per-variable methods), Gen.jl (trace operations as a user toolkit) and Pyro (handlers and guides as programs).
3. The systems people call "natural" for model definition are PyMC (named dims and data containers), Stan (blocks and
   constraints), and Infer.NET (ranges and gates): all three have a declarative, inspectable model value.
4. The one family omai had only in special cases when this was written is message passing on arbitrary exponential-family factor
   graphs (Infer.NET's core). It had discrete BP, Gaussian BP, discrete and Gaussian-latent EP, and discrete VMP, TRW and mean field.
   Since then a model written as a spec is compiled to such a factor graph and run by EP, VMP or BP (`ep.md`, `ep-status.md`).
5. The structural idea with the most leverage across all of them is automatic exact marginalisation of the conjugate and
   linear-Gaussian parts (Birch delayed sampling, JAGS conjugate samplers, Pyro `collapse`, Infer.NET message operators),
   combined with a sampler or optimiser for the rest.
6. Validation culture differs: Stan and ArviZ users expect SBC, rank plots and reference posteriors; Infer.NET and Pyro
   users expect agreement with exact answers on tractable models. The plan does both.
