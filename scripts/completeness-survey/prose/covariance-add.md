## What to add: ranked, in batches one agent could take

Directional, by the survey's rule: how many users expect the thing, how well a stepped, traced version shows what omai is for, and how cheap it is.
Sizes: S a day or less, M a few days, L a week or more. Homes are proposals under `packages/engine/src/lib` and follow the shape of the folder
(`covariance/` gains files; `docs/structure.md` section 3 and `make barrels` need an entry when one is built). Everything is a library module with tests, TSDoc and an
`@index` entry; nothing here is for the gallery except where marked "new render capability", and a poc only when the owner asks. Names are checked, behaviour is not.

### Where a stepped, traced version teaches something a one-shot function cannot

- The Marchenko-Pastur eigenvalue plot with denoising, and the BBP transition (gap 1): a spike is invisible below `1 + sqrt(q)` and escapes the bulk above it.
- The graphical-lasso path with the stepped solver (gap 2): the sweep, the duality gap, the edges entering as the penalty falls, and the choice of the penalty by three rules.
- Riemannian geometry of SPD matrices (gap 7): the geodesic between two ellipses and the Karcher mean converging step by step.
- Shrinkage intensity as a slider (gap 4): the eigenvalue spectrum, the condition number and the held-out likelihood against the intensity.
- Nearest correlation matrix by Dykstra, the Tyler fixed point, the flip-flop for a matrix-normal and the EM covariance with missing values: four short iterations whose convergence is the figure.
- Principal component pursuit (later): a video-like matrix splitting into low rank and sparse parts as the ADMM iterates.
- Time-varying graphical lasso and DCC correlation (later): a graph or a correlation that moves.
- New render capabilities these would need, to be checked against the existing recipes before a recipe is added: an overlay of a density on a histogram (the eigenvalues), a correlation matrix in clustered order with the dendrogram, and a cell map (a heatmap of flagged cells for cellwise robustness).

### What this survey could not see

- Behaviour. All verdicts are by name; an omai function can be thinner than its namesake (the graphical lasso has the algorithm of sklearn but not its `GraphicalLassoCV`; the 16 of sklearn.covariance were counted from names, and one of them, `GraphicalLassoCV`, did not survive a second look).
- The R packages were read as index pages, not as source, and robustbase only to the first 100,000 characters of its index. Several R packages with a Python peer that is not installable (for instance `mgarch`-style DCC packages) were not looked at; `arch` was read and has no multivariate model.
- skggm has no wheel and was read from its sdist; it is not maintained. gglasso, regain, robpy and rmt_denoise are small research packages, so a gap against one of them is a gap against a method, not against a standard.
- The concept lists (sparse and structured, graph methods, tests, random matrix theory, missing data, time-varying, SPD concepts, and the pandas, river, JavaScript and PyMC lists) were written from memory. They list methods a reader would recognise; they are not inventories of a release, and a short list means the author remembered little, not that little exists.
- Nothing about numerical agreement: omai's Ledoit-Wolf, OAS, FastMCD and graphical lasso have golden tests against scikit-learn in the repository, and that is the only agreement evidence this survey relies on.
